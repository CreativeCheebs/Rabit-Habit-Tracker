## Habit returns and curve evidence

Research supports an asymptotic increase in habit automaticity with repeated behavior in a consistent context. In Lally et al.'s real-world study, an asymptotic curve fit many participants' automaticity data; the time to reach 95% of the modeled plateau varied widely, and missing one opportunity did not materially affect the formation process. A later review of tracking methods describes exponential-asymptotic and logistic curve proposals, while noting disagreement about the trajectory and a need for more research.

That evidence describes **habit strength/automaticity**, not a universal curve for converting impact points or consistency into future “returns.” The first version therefore uses transparent, explicit impact points and compares raw point totals. It does not invent a logarithmic, exponential, or other return multiplier. If return modeling is explored later, it should be clearly labeled as a personal, data-backed estimate rather than an established general rule.
